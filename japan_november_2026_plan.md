## How Codex should use this file

This is the durable planning source of truth for the trip. Treat it as working context, not a finished travel article.

When updating it:

1. Preserve confirmed bookings, prices, points, dates, constraints, and decision rationale.
2. Do not silently replace or delete rejected alternatives; move them to **Deprioritized / rejected** and retain the reason.
3. Separate known facts from assumptions and time-sensitive items that still require verification.
4. Update the relevant section and the change log whenever a decision is made.
5. Prefer a strong, realistic trip over maximizing the number of destinations or “value” on paper.
6. Avoid overscheduling. Build days from a few anchors plus nearby optional modules.
7. Use Google Maps or official links for navigation and live hours; this file should preserve the planning logic.
8. Before recommending a points transfer, verify live award space, exact mileage, fees, aircraft/product, airport, and cancellation rules. Transfers are not assumed reversible.

## Trip principles and preferences

- Japan is visited regularly; the trip does not need to cover standard first-time highlights.
- Favor distinctive local food, specialty coffee, design/retail, Japanese outdoor gear, neighborhood wandering, and seasonal experiences.
- Casual, local “food of the people” is generally more appealing than nightlife or drinking-focused plans.
- Hotel changes are acceptable when they create meaningfully different experiences, but unnecessary one-night detours are not.
- A day trip to Kamakura should be a real day—gear, food, and place—not merely a retail errand.
- Autumn foliage is relevant, especially in Kyoto, but exact timing must be treated as forecast-dependent.
- Return-flight comfort matters. Economy is not acceptable; realistic alternatives are business-class awards or paid premium economy.
- Chase Ultimate Rewards are usually most useful through Hyatt. Bilt points are a plausible way to solve the harder return-flight problem.

## Alternatives and decision history

### Hyatt Regency Kyoto — **REJECTED**

- Cost was 29,000 Hyatt points per night versus 9,500 for Hyatt Place.
- Two-night premium: 39,000 points.
- Regency is the nicer full-service hotel, but the improvement did not justify roughly triple the points for this itinerary.
- Hyatt Place has a functional location and preserves points for stays where the hotel materially changes the experience.
- Do not reopen this decision unless pricing changes dramatically or Hyatt Place becomes unavailable.

### Return to Tokyo on Nov 20 — **DEPRIORITIZED**

- Would leave Osaka as essentially a one-night hotel stop.
- The trip already has substantial Tokyo time.
- Adds a long-distance transit day without solving a meaningful problem.

### Hiroshima, Kanazawa, or another major one-night destination — **DEPRIORITIZED**

- Adds hotel and train logistics for too little usable time.
- Risks itinerary-maximizing instead of experience-maximizing.
- Reconsider only if there is one specific, compelling experience—not generic destination collecting.

### Nara, Kobe, Uji, or a distinctive nearby overnight — **OPTIONAL / NOT LEADING**

- Geographically more plausible than a distant one-night city.
- Still needs a compelling reason to beat a second Osaka night.
- Day-trip treatment may be better than adding another hotel change.

### Paid JAL or ANA premium economy — **BACKUP**

- Acceptable cabin; compare only if JAL business awards disappear or fees/transfer conditions are poor.
- Cash pricing must be compared with the real opportunity cost of 140,000 Bilt points, not a simplistic cents-per-point calculation.

### Positioning via Taipei, Seoul, or another Asian city — **BACKUP / HIGH FRICTION**

- Potentially useful if it unlocks meaningfully better business-class value.
- Must account for positioning fare, baggage, connection risk, extra hotel/time, airport transfers, and reduced Japan time.
- Do not pursue creativity for its own sake.
